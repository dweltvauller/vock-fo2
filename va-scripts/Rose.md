# Rose — VA Script
**Character:** Rose, Owner of Rose's Bed and Breakfast (Modoc)

**Total recordable lines:** 37 (`rose1`–`rose37`)

---

> **Direction:** An older woman, plump, gray-haired, warm and motherly — the kind of innkeeper who calls everyone "dear" or "sweetie" and means it. She runs her diner with brisk, cheerful efficiency and takes real pride in her cooking, but there's a dry, deadpan streak underneath — she'll describe something faintly horrifying (what's actually in a canis burger, harvesting brahmin scrotum for the fries contest) in exactly the same pleasant hostess voice she uses for everything else. Genuinely warm and generous toward a hungry or down-on-their-luck traveler. If she catches someone repeatedly digging through her lost-and-found box, that warmth curdles fast into suspicion and then open hostility. If her husband Cornelius has died, she's a different person entirely — quiet, grieving, business shuttered.

---

## First Greeting

*Rose welcomes a new customer to the diner — the line depends on whether she's speaking to a man or a woman.*

`rose1:` Welcome to Rose's Bed and Breakfast. My, aren't you the strapping young man. What can Rose help you with today?

`rose2:` Welcome to Rose's Bed and Breakfast. Oh my, what a pretty young lady you are. What can Rose do for you today?

## Returning Customer

`rose3:` Glad to see you again! What can I do for you?

## Closed — Grieving

*Plays instead of any normal greeting once Cornelius has died. She won't engage beyond this.*

`rose34:` I'm sorry, we're closed. There's been a death in the family. [sniff]

## Welcoming the Hero of Modoc

*Once the Ghost Farm mystery has been solved. The first line plays the first time she sees the PC afterward; the second on every visit after that.*

`rose4:` Oh my! It's you. Everyone is talking about how you risked your life for us, and solved the mystery of the Ghost Farm. Know that you are always welcome here, and of course all your meals are on the house. You may also rummage through the lost and found box and take whatever you wish as a special reward.

`rose5:` It's the hero of Modoc! Welcome back.

## Taking an Order

*She points the customer at the menu — again, a slightly different line for a man versus a woman.*

`rose6:` That's what I like to hear. A growing young boy needs to eat to keep up his strength, and I can tell you haven't been eating properly. You just sit right down and look at the menu.

`rose7:` And so you should be. When was the last time you had a good meal, young lady? Why, you're as thin as a rail. You just look at the menu and tell Rose what you would like to eat.

## Not Enough Caps

`rose8:` Oh, I'm sorry, but you don't seem to have enough money. Why don't you come back later?

## The Brahmin Fries Eating Contest

*A running gag: order the brahmin fries and Rose offers a shot at breaking the house record. Play the whole scene with real hostess enthusiasm, egging the PC on right up through the gross-out punchline.*

`rose9:` Back for some more, I see. You must really like brahmin fries. I'm sorry; since you hold the record, you can't enter the contest again until somebody else breaks your score.

`rose10:` Brahmin fries it is, then. We have a contest on who can eat the most Brahmin fries; so far, the record is 36. If you break the record, you don't have to pay the tab for your meal. You look like you'd be able to beat the record with no problem. Are you interested in trying?

`rose11:` Oh, that's just grand. Each order consists of 10 pieces. I'll have to ask you for the money up front. I'll refund it as soon as you break the record. That'll be $20, please.

`rose12:` [Rose takes the money and comes back with a heaping platter of brahmin fries. They are not quite what you expected. They appear to be about the size of golf balls, covered in batter and fried in oil to a deep, golden brown. You take a tentative bite of one of the fries. These are delicious! You start into the meal with zeal.]

`rose13:` [At around the 34th fry, you start to feel the effects of overeating. A crowd has gathered to witness the historic event of the old record being broken. In an attempt to regain your composure, you strike up a conversation with Rose.]

`rose14:` [You pop a fry into your mouth.] 'Mmff... splt... mnch...' Don't talk with your mouth full, dear. [You swallow.]

`rose15:` Oh, it's no secret. You just dip them in batter, add some salt and fry in grease. [You pop another one in your mouth.]

`rose16:` That's 37! You just broke the record! [The crowd cheers!]

`rose17:` [The crowd yells, "38!"] Well, what makes ours taste so special is that you have to harvest it while the brahmin is still alive. You just grab the brahmin by the scrotum and 'snip' four with one cut. You've got be careful; the brahmin usually gets riled up at that point.

`rose18:` We can't count that last one, but you broke the record at 37! Here's your money back. Congratulations!

`rose19:` Ah, that's my specialty. I hope you're hungry; I guarantee you've never tasted an omelet like this one anywhere. [She leaves and returns with the biggest damn omelet you've ever seen.]

## Too Busy to Chat

*Her default brush-off the first time someone tries to ask questions before ordering anything.*

`rose21:` I'm sorry, sweetie, I'm quite busy serving customers. Maybe if you ordered something I'll have time to chat with you between orders.

*Once the PC has actually ordered something, she's willing to talk between customers.*

`rose22:` Sure, but make it quick, dear. I don't want to keep the other customers waiting.

## Answering Questions — Cornelius's Missing Watch

`rose23:` Oh dear! Have you been talking to my husband Cornelius? This whole business with the watch is just terrible. Cornelius has got the whole town believing that his best friend, Farrel, took his watch. If you've talked to him, I'm sure you've noticed his mind isn't quite functioning properly. I'm pretty sure the poor dear just left the watch somewhere and forgot where.

## Wrapping Up a Chat

*Plays after any of the question topics below, once she's ready to get back to work.*

`rose24:` Now, if you don't need anything else, I have other customers to serve.

## Answering Questions — the GECK

`rose25:` Did you say GECK? No, I can't say that I have. I have heard of good soup recipe that requires three geckos. But no, I haven't heard of anything about a GECK.

## Answering Questions — the Ghost Farm

*She shudders at the subject.*

`rose26:` [She shivers involuntarily.] Stay away from there! I've heard tales from travelers about rotting bodies tied to stakes, and glowing shapes moving through the fields at night. I don't know how much of this is true, but I do know a while back Karl moved out there and hasn't been seen since.

## Answering Questions — the Desert Omelet

*She gets visibly uncomfortable partway through and changes the subject.*

`rose27:` Ah, you've had the desert omelet. Delicious, wasn't it? Some people say it has healing properties. [She looks around nervously.] Oh! Some customers just came in. I'll be right back. [Hmm... She never answered your question.]

## Answering Questions — What's in a Canis Burger

*Deadpan — she has no idea this is unsettling.*

`rose28:` Our burgers are made of nothing but the choicest, select, grade-A dog. The meat is ground up and made into half-pound patties. And to give it that unique smoky flavor, they are slow grilled over dried brahmin dung.

## Feeding a Hungry Traveler

*Rose takes pity on someone who's clearly starving and feeds them for free.*

`rose29:` You poor thing. You look half starved. Come sit down and let Rose get you something to eat. [She leaves and returns with a large omelet.] Now, you don't move from that spot until you finish every bit of that.

## Selling Water

`rose31:` Really!? You must be really thirsty. I raised the price to discourage customers from ordering it. Drinking water is at a premium out here, with the lack of rain and the well drying up. Do you still want some?

## No More Omelets

*An accident in the kitchen has taken the omelet off the menu.*

`rose32:` I'm sorry, we stopped serving omelets. Some fool broke into our coop and killed our... chicken.

## A Pricey Glass of Water

*Plays instead of the usual order line when someone actually pays the steep $1000 asking price for water — she throws in a cookie as a small, slightly bemused reward.*

`rose33:` [She quickly returns with a glass of some brownish liquid substance. It appears that there is more dirt than water in the glass. However, you are so thirsty that you drink it anyway.] I brought a cookie for you to enjoy with that, sweetie.

## Warning Off a Thief

*Rose keeps a lost-and-found box behind the counter for herself alone. If someone keeps helping themselves to it, she calls out with mounting suspicion and anger — these escalate in order, ending right before she turns hostile.*

`rose35:` Hey! I'm the only one allowed to go through the lost and found box.

`rose36:` I'm beginning to think you're a thief!

`rose37:` Thief! Thief! Thief!
